Fundamentals

What Stablecoins Actually Are

Stablecoins (payment stablecoins) are tokenized monetary instruments designed to maintain a stable value — typically 1:1 with a fiat currency — via reserve backing and redemption rights. Regulators emphasize that "stablecoin" is a market term, not a guarantee of stability.

They're increasingly used as: payment rails (cross-border, merchant, B2B treasury — 24/7, programmable, faster finality), settlement assets (tokenized cash legs for tokenized securities), collateral (on-chain margining, automated substitution, real-time haircuts), and tokenized deposits (banks exploring deposit-like tokens for closed/open networks).

Taxonomy

Types, Collateral & Risk Profiles

USDC (Circle) USDT (Tether) PYUSD (PayPal)

1:1 backed by fiat and cash-equivalent reserves (T-bills, repo, bank deposits). Mint on deposit, burn on redemption. USDC reserves in segregated accounts + BlackRock Circle Reserve Fund. USDT includes commercial paper.

Stablecoin Lifecycle
MINT (fiat → stablecoin):
  1. Wire $1M to issuer bank  2. AML/KYC check
  3. mint(address, 1_000_000e6)  4. USDC in wallet
  // Latency: T+0 to T+1 (wire speed)

BURN (stablecoin → fiat):
  1. Call redeem(1_000_000e6)  2. Tokens burned on-chain
  3. Wire from reserve account  4. $1M in bank
  // Latency: T+0 to T+1

TRANSFER (on-chain): transfer(recipient, amount)
  // Seconds · $0.01–$2.00 · 24/7/365

DAI / USDS (MakerDAO) LUSD (Liquity)

Over-collateralized (150-200%) by crypto locked in smart contract vaults. Automated liquidation if collateral drops. Fully transparent on-chain. Capital-inefficient; vulnerable to cascade liquidations. Generally not primary stablecoin for regulated institutions.

⚠ High risk — not for institutional use

Terra/UST collapse (May 2022): $18B algorithmic stablecoin → $0. "Death spiral" where redemptions caused hyperinflation of paired token. Demonstrated fundamental fragility. Proposed U.S. legislation (GENIUS/STABLE Acts) prohibits algorithmic stablecoins from qualifying as permitted payment stablecoins. Your platform should maintain an allowlist architecture that blocks these by default.

JPM Coin EURCV (SocGen FORGE)

Bank-issued token = bank liability, potentially deposit insurance eligible. Preserves deposit relationship, existing banking regulation, balance sheet economics. Not freely transferable — moves within closed networks. Closer to faster bank-to-bank settlement than universal digital dollar.

Regulated Liability Network (RLN)

Concept tested by Citi, HSBC, Mastercard: shared ledger with tokenized deposits from multiple banks + tokenized central bank liability. Preserves two-tier banking on shared rails.

Digital Euro (ECB) e-CNY (PBoC)

Central bank liability — highest-quality money. Wholesale (interbank settlement) vs. retail (consumer use). Engineering patterns overlap significantly with stablecoin infrastructure.

Technical Architecture

Inside a Fiat-Backed Stablecoin

Solidity — FiatToken (USDC v2.1 simplified)
contract FiatToken is ERC20, Pausable, Blacklistable {
    address public masterMinter;   // controls minting
    address public pauser;         // emergency pause
    address public blacklister;    // OFAC compliance
    address public rescuer;        // recover stuck tokens

    // Blacklist: freeze sanctioned addresses instantly
    function blacklist(address acct) external onlyBlacklister {
        blacklisted[acct] = true;
    }
    // Transfer: checks blacklist + pause state
    function transfer(address to, uint256 val) public override
        whenNotPaused notBlacklisted(msg.sender) notBlacklisted(to)
        returns (bool) { return super.transfer(to, val); }
    // Pause: halt ALL transfers globally
    function pause() external onlyPauser { _pause(); }
}
Critical for engineers: administrative functions

Fiat-backed stablecoins have blacklist (freeze any address), pause (halt all transfers globally), and seizure capabilities. Your integration must handle: what if a customer's USDC is frozen? What if all transfers pause for hours? Design graceful degradation paths.

Impact on Risk Functions

How Stablecoins Change Every Risk Domain

Stablecoins don't just add new risks — they change how existing risk functions operate. Here's the complete risk-function map.

06a Credit Risk — Who Is the Obligor?

▼

Holding stablecoins: Your exposure is to the issuer/reserve structure + enforceability of redemption rights.

Lending against tokenized collateral: You inherit liquidation mechanics (smart contract execution, oracle pricing, on-chain market depth).

Credit to fintech using stablecoins: Monitor asset-liability mismatch, run sensitivity, reliance on single issuer/chain.

Credit events look different: A "depeg" can be a credit signal (reserve impairment, redemption friction, legal injunction) even before realized losses.

06b Market Risk — Beyond Price Volatility

▼

• Basis risk — Gap between stablecoin price and reference currency (temporary or persistent)

• Liquidity/price impact — On-chain liquidity can evaporate quickly in stressed conditions

• Oracle risk — Market values determined by feeds that can lag, be manipulated, or fail

06c Liquidity Risk (ALM) — The Most Immediate Concern

▼

Stablecoins create faster, always-on "digital runs." Instant redemption 24/7 shortens time to manage outflows. If issuing a stablecoin or tokenized deposit, ALM must assume stress outflows at any time, including weekends. Intraday liquidity management becomes continuous. Basel cryptoasset standards influence treatment and disclosures (Jan 2026).

06d Model Risk (MRM)

▼

Stablecoins create/amplify models in: blockchain transaction monitoring (risk scoring, clustering, velocity), liquidity/run risk (redemption forecasting, depeg stress), market risk (haircuts, oracle reliability), credit (counterparty exposure, wrong-way risk).

Distinct model risks: Label leakage and feedback loops (bad actors adapt). Vendor-model dependence (many AML analytics are black box). Data lineage issues from forks, reorgs, address attribution uncertainty.

06e Finance, Accounting & Treasury

▼

Recognition & measurement depend on legal form (cash equivalent vs financial asset vs intangible). Reserve accounting and attestation/audit become part of financial reporting controls. Revenue recognition: transaction fees, float/rebate structures require policy clarity. Circle earned ~$1.7B in reserve income in 2024.

Treasury: Intraday liquidity management becomes continuous. Atomic settlement reduces some risks but introduces protocol/contract dependency.

Regulatory Framework

Compliance Landscape

GENIUS Act (Senate): Establishes framework for "permitted payment stablecoin issuers" — banks/credit unions and certain nonbanks under oversight. Requires 1:1 reserves (cash, T-bills, repos), monthly attestations, annual audits. Prohibits algorithmic stablecoins.

STABLE Act (House): Similar framework with differences in state vs. federal pathways.

Engineering impact: Reserve management systems must track composition in real-time, generate attestation reports, enforce asset-type restrictions.

MiCA (in effect since June 2024 for stablecoins) explicitly classifies:

• E-Money Tokens (EMTs): Pegged to single fiat. Must be issued by authorized credit institution or e-money institution. 1:1 reserves. Direct redemption rights.

• Asset-Referenced Tokens (ARTs): Pegged to basket. Stricter: own funds, reserve management, governance.

Transaction monitoring: On-chain patterns — structuring, layering through wallets, mixer interaction, high-risk jurisdictions. Tools: Chainalysis, TRM Labs, Elliptic.

Travel Rule: Transfers ≥$3,000 require originator/beneficiary info. Protocols: TRISA, Notabene, Shyft, Sygna.

OFAC: Screen every wallet address against SDN list pre-transaction. OFAC has designated specific Ethereum addresses.

Self-hosted wallets: No counterparty for Travel Rule data. Enhanced due diligence required.

Risk-Based De-Peg Playbook

Scenario Stablecoin Depegs to $0.95

▼

Immediate (0-1 hr): Automated monitoring triggers alert. Halt new customer deposits in affected stablecoin. Switch payment routing to alternative stablecoin (if multi-coin supported). Notify sponsor bank (if BaaS).

Assessment (1-4 hr): Determine cause: market panic vs. reserve impairment vs. issuer insolvency. Check issuer attestation status. Evaluate on-chain redemption queue depth.

Escalation (4-24 hr): If reserve impairment suspected: initiate redemption of institution's holdings. If prolonged: trigger contingency plan for customer communications. Consider SAR filing if fraud indicators present.

Recovery: Post-incident review. Update risk limits. Adjust stablecoin allowlist if needed. Report to board risk committee.

Getting started checklist

(1) Choose stablecoin — USDC default for regulated use. (2) Choose chain — start with one L2 (Base/Arbitrum). (3) Wallet infra — Circle Programmable Wallets or Fireblocks. (4) Compliance screening — Chainalysis or TRM. (5) Event processing — webhook handler + ledger sync. (6) Testnet first. (7) Chain/asset allowlist policy. (8) 24/7 incident playbooks.
